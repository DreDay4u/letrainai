## The Problem

A home-services company with six employees was losing work it never knew it had. Customers — most of them shopping around between providers — sent inquiries through the website and by text, and then waited. During business hours, someone usually replied within a few hours. Evenings and weekends, inquiries sat unanswered until the next morning, by which time a meaningful share of customers had already booked with someone else.

For a local business, the customer who doesn't get a reply isn't a lost conversation. It's a lost job — and a customer who may never call again.

## The Approach

We started with the single highest-leverage process: responding to inquiries. The metric was first response time — how quickly a customer gets an answer, measured around the clock.

The approach paired AI triage with human escalation rules. The AI's role was narrow and deliberate: answer the questions that come up in nearly every inquiry — pricing ranges, availability, service areas — and book appointments when the customer is ready. Anything outside that scope, or any customer who explicitly wanted a person, was escalated immediately. The team stayed in control of everything that mattered; the AI handled everything that repeated.

## The Solution

An AI response system now answers incoming inquiries instantly, in the company's voice, 24 hours a day. It provides the information customers ask for, checks availability against the team's calendar, and books appointments directly. Complex requests — unusual jobs, complaints, multi-service questions — are escalated to staff with full context attached, so no conversation starts over from zero.

## The Results

- First response time dropped from **4+ hours to under 2 minutes**, around the clock
- After-hours inquiries stopped leaking: bookings that previously waited until morning now happen the moment the customer is ready
- Staff handle only the complex conversations — the ones that actually need a human — while the routine volume resolves itself

The company didn't add a receptionist. It added availability. For a local business, being the one that replies first is often the difference between winning the job and not being considered at all.
