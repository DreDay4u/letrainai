Every business has busywork. Data entry, order processing, invoice chasing, report building, status updates — the tasks everyone complains about and nobody budgets for. They're not in your P&L as a line item, so they feel free. They are not.

Here's the uncomfortable math: if you can't name what your manual processes cost, you can't make a rational decision about automating them. The good news is that you can calculate it in about 20 minutes with a piece of paper and four honest estimates.

## Step 1: List your repetitive tasks

Walk a typical week and write down every task that is done the same way, more than once a week, by a human. Be specific: "entering orders from the marketplace into the ERP," not "admin work." Most SMBs land on four to eight tasks. If you can't name five, ask your team — they know exactly what eats their week.

## Step 2: Estimate hours per week per task

For each task, estimate how many hours your team collectively spends on it per week. Be honest, and round up — people systematically underestimate their own busywork. If eight people each spend an hour a day on something, that's 40 hours a week, not "a little time here and there."

## Step 3: Multiply by your loaded labor cost

The loaded cost of an employee is their salary plus benefits, taxes, and overhead — for SMB employees, realistically **$35–$60 per hour**. Don't use the minimum wage you pay; use what the time actually costs you, including the person's full burden. The loaded rate is the number that makes automation decisions honest.

## Step 4: Multiply by 50 working weeks

Take your weekly hours, multiply by your loaded hourly rate, then multiply by 50 (the working weeks in a year, allowing for holidays and leave). The result is the annual cost of that one task.

## A worked example

A 12-person team spending 10 hours/week on data entry at $45/hr loaded cost:

10 hours × $45 × 50 weeks = **$23,400/year — and that's just one task.**

Add in the three or four other repetitive tasks on your list and you'll almost certainly find a five-figure annual cost sitting quietly in your operations. Often six. That's not overhead — that's an investment opportunity.
